# Ember & Ash — how to play the complete loop

This guide walks through every connected system in one or two in-game weeks. Normal play needs no special tools. For quick evaluation, the **Developer Tools** panel (F10, testing only) can speed up any step.

## Controls

| Key | Action |
|---|---|

| E / Space | Interact, or press in a skill check |
| I | Stores and equipment |

## Week one: the workshop

### 1. Start the forge

1. Talk to Borin in the stock hall.
2. Craft an **Iron Longsword**: Begin it at the Forge, then follow the marker to the anvil, quench trough and grinding wheel.
   - The grinding wheel is the *Edge Control* minigame.
   - Roll the dice digitally or enter physical rolls.
3. The results panel shows every operation, the dice, the stats and the rarity score.

### 2. Put people to work (H)

- Leave Borin mining. Use the presets, or split his time with the sliders.
- On day 2 or 3, send him to **Ironroot Mine**. The ledger shows the risk and odds before you commit.
- He returns 1–2 days later. His bag waits on the workshop floor.
- Open it: items are revealed one at a time, rarer finds more slowly, and legendaries flash orange with a star and their own sound.

### 3. Process what comes home

| Material | What to do with it |
|---|---|
| Ore | Smelt it at the crucible and forge |
| Gems | Set them in a **Garnet Ring** (rune stabilization at the engraving stand) |
| Unidentified relics | Select them in your stores (I) → **Study the relic**. They turn out to be ancient arms, rare components or forgotten recipes. Read a recipe to learn it, for example the **Starsteel Blade**. |
| Draughts, oils and coatings | Put them on the shop shelf. Adventurers buy draughts before they leave. Coatings and oils treat a blade at the **Infusion Rack**. |

### 4. Trade and scout

- Send a hand with a good Travelling skill down **Highmarsh Road** with a 50–200 g purse.
- They buy materials from the suppliers you know, and sell anything you marked *Send with the next trade run*.
- Scouting a region lowers its danger for everyone. The first scouting trip can reveal a new supplier: mithril, gems or ethereal thread.

### 5. Recruit (notice board, village square)

- New applicants arrive every week, and some weeks nobody comes.
- Up to three hands. When the roll is full, replace someone directly from the board.

## Week two: the village and the adventurers

### 6. Customers

- Customers come only during opening hours. They sit on the four seats outside the shop door, and you are told only once they arrive.
- Talk to a waiting customer to:
  - serve them
  - take a repair job, including adventurers' own worn gear
  - sell them a draught
  - ask them to leave
- Your stock is laid out on the shop's display table.

### 7. Equip an adventurer (J, O)

1. Check the Adventurers overview. What you know grows with familiarity.
2. Take an **order**. Orders follow the threats of the adventurer's next trip: bound for the Barrow Fields, they want **fire**.
3. Forge to the order, deliver it, and watch them leave through the village exit.
4. When they return, their story in the overview names the gear that mattered. Good gear wins fights; poor gear gets people hurt or killed.

### 8. Consequences

- If your work fails someone, they come back disputing it.
- Refunding earns respect. Refusing earns a grudge that spreads to their family: they won't buy or commission, and someone may egg your shopfront.
- Reputation (ledger, L) changes how busy the shop is and what prices people accept.
- Aldric the grocer delivers weekly. What he brings, and whether he hands you a runestone he took as payment, depends on how you treat his daughter Mira.

### 9. The inn (evenings)

- Sit in on the **table talk**: the room discusses what really happened, including your work.
- Play **knucklebones**.
- Upstairs is the Velvet Lantern (fade-to-black, with rumours that sharpen the Adventurers overview).

### 10. Romance and marriage (Mira, Sigrun, Elowen or Kestra)

1. Gifts and talk build affection.
2. Two drinks by the fire lead to **dating**.
3. A walk by the stream at dusk tells you her story.
4. Ask to court her.
5. Another evening brings her **keepsake**.
6. A ring from your own anvil, then propose.

Married, she keeps adventuring:

- your gear is free for her
- half her spoils and some of her loot come home
- she sleeps in your quarters
- her life still depends on what you forge

### 11. Masters of the forge

- Upgrades (L) widen precision zones: the Alchemist's Kit and Runewright's Tools.
- The **Arcane Foundry** unlocks at Runecraft 3, once mithril has been found, for 600 g.
- Arcane chain: **Charge Adamantine**, then the **Adamantine Warplate**, with a ward bound into the metal itself.
- The **Emberwarden Longsword** uses four disciplines in one blade: steel, an ember rune, a flame-quenched edge and a ghost-thread grip. Give it to an adventurer heading for the barrows.

## The living village: following the adventurers

**Difficulty** is chosen at New Game and can be changed any time from the menu (Esc → Difficulty):

| | Apprentice | Journeyman | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Death | never — the fallen are carried home | possible, rare | real |
| Live observation | yes, full detail | yes, full detail | no — you hear what happened when they return |
| Enemies / healing | ×0.8 / ×1.5 | ×1 / ×1 | ×1.25 / ×0.75 |
| Prices / wages / loot | ×1.15 / ×0.85 / ×1.2 | ×1 | ×0.9 / ×1.15 / ×0.85 |

**Every day:** at 06:00 adventurers gather at tables in the Wyvern and form parties of one to four; at 07:00 they buy supplies; at 08:00 they leave through a gate for Whisperwood, Ironroot Mine, Highmarsh Road, the Barrow Fields or Deep Ironroot. The expedition then unfolds on the clock — travel, scouting, traps, chests, shrines, travellers, fights and a boss — whether or not you watch.

**The MOST WANTED board** (in the inn by the door, or press **B**):

- *Most Wanted* — a pinned sheet per adventurer, ranked by renown. Click a sheet for the **dossier**: condition, attributes, defence and to-hit, skills, relationships, record, and the **actual items they carry** — hover an item for its maker and history.
- *Active Expeditions* — parties forming and on the road, with **Observe Expedition**.
- *At the Inn* — who is home, resting or injured.
- *Legends & History* — the hall of renown, the fallen, tales from the chronicle and past expeditions (**Revisit** replays them).

**Observe Expedition** shows the region map with the route, the points of interest and the party marker moving along the road; the members' health, fatigue and morale; and a timestamped journal. Each fight, trap and chest has an **Inspect** button that replays it: every d20 roll, the modifier, the target's defence, damage broken into weapon, attribute, class favour and armour, all naming the item instance used. Observing never pauses or changes anything: use **P** and **−/+** (or the buttons in the observer) to control time yourself.

**What comes home:** gold, experience and renown; finds offered to you at the shop counter (Customers & Offers — Buy or Decline within three days); broken weapons brought in for repair; items renamed after a boss falls ("The Wolfbane"); and on bad days, the missing — who may stagger home, be found by a later party, or be confirmed dead.

**Commissions** now state a priority — *Quality first*, *Needed fast* or *Tight purse* — and a bonus objective (craftsmanship 75%+, delivered by tomorrow, or rare quality) that pays extra and is remembered.

**Employees** have a hidden potential that changes how fast they learn. It is revealed after about five days of work (shown in the Hirelings ledger). On Master, applicants' skills on the notice are only a range.

**Talk:** ask an adventurer about their last expedition — they tell it from the real journal, and mention your weapon if it served them.

## Iteration 009: from a village forge to dragon armour

**You start small.** The Ironhand Smithy is one corner of a large old building: a shopfront, a smoky smithing
hall with a forge, anvil, crucible, quench trough and grinding wheel, a narrow stock hall and a back room with a cot.
The rest belongs to your neighbours — Hesketh the cobbler, Marla's laundry, Ilse the weaver, the chandler's store,
a sealed dye-works vault and the Ashby family's apartment. You can see their rooms through locked doors.

**Learn (Skill Tree — toolbar or the station panel).** Six branches: Traditional Smithing, Mining & Metallurgy,
Runecrafting, Alchemy, Leatherworking & Enchanted Stitching, and the sealed Arcane Metalworking. Each node shows
exactly what it needs (skill level, earlier nodes, knowledge, an installed station, materials you have held,
tuition) and what it unlocks. Skills rise by crafting. Secret recipes stay hidden until you find their notes.

**Equip the workshop (Workshop button, or the deeds on the shelf in your back room).**
- *Equipment*: buy stations the tree allows; they are delivered to storage.
- *Workshop layout*: place, move or turn stations; take down or put up internal walls; cut or brick up doorways;
  change a room's use (workshop or living space). Stations stand only in workshop rooms, a few per room — when the
  smithing hall is full you need more space. Every change is checked live: every room, station, the counter and a
  bed must stay reachable from the front door, and the shopfront keeps its walls.
- *Property*: buy a neighbour's room — only one that adjoins yours. It turns from their home or shop into yours.

**Materials.** The herbalist (beside the inn) sells herbs and a little aether dust each morning.
At the harbour (south-east) merchant ships call every few days — a spice trader, a dwarven ore barge and a
far-north whaler with hides and, rarely, dragon scales. The harbourmaster takes **special import** orders for
anything you have seen or know of: pay 40% now, the rest on delivery. Hirelings bring ore, hides and relics home;
adventurers offer their finds at your counter.

**The road to dragon armour.** Iron longsword → steelworking → runes (restore an old rune tablet to learn rune lore,
then create original runes) → mithril and the Arcane Crucible (buy the vault or the Arcane Foundry upgrade) → mithril
plating → exotic hides and dragon-scale preparation (hold a real dragon scale) → Legendary Arcane Craftsmanship →
**Dragonscale Armour** (four scales, two bones, wyrm leather and orichalcum).

**Plan before you forge (Crafting button → Equipment Designer).** Pick an item type, a material, a rune and a
treatment; the designer shows the expected stats and a checklist of what is missing.

**Skill checks** now explain themselves the first time (with a practice run at tutorial difficulty), close by
themselves after the result, and scale with your skill, the recipe and the station. An *assist* setting in Settings
makes every check gentler.

**Customers** sit down outside before you are told they are waiting, and say exactly what they want: the item,
the attribute, the material, the budget and the reward.

**You get tired.** Crafting and long days build fatigue (the Energy bar under the clock); a tired smith's checks
are harder. Rest by the hearth or in bed. Change your look at the mirror in your quarters (or Esc → Appearance).

**Ironfront.** Barnaby Quill runs the village's craze in his basement war-room (his house on the north lane): a collectible
card game of "fantastical" machines — Tiger tanks, Spitfires, Katyushas. Build a deck, play standard or
**Blitzkrieg** against Jory, Barnaby, Pip and Sigrun, win cards and buy packs.

**Help.** The bottom toolbar opens everything; F1 opens a searchable reference (no spoilers); M shows the map; the
minimap marks people who want a word with you; History buttons show the story of a person, an item or a station.

### A 20-minute test route
1. New Game → Journeyman. Look around the smithing hall; open the Workshop plan (deeds on the back-room shelf).
2. Forge an iron longsword (forge → anvil → quench trough → grinding wheel). The first check shows its how-to card.
3. Open the Skill Tree: learn Mail, Plate & Silverwork, Common Ores, Ordinary Alloys (F10 → +1000 gold if needed).
4. Workshop → Equipment: buy the bellows; Workshop layout: place it in the smithing hall; Apply.
5. Property: buy Hesketh's Cobblery; layout: put up a wall, cut a doorway, Apply — walk into the new room.
6. Visit the herbalist and the harbour (F10 → "Ship docks now"); place a special import.
7. Barnaby's house → stairs → play a game of Ironfront.
8. Save, quit, Continue: rooms, walls, stations, tree, decks and imports are as you left them.

## Iteration 010: adventurers, equipment, a living village

**Getting around.** Click the ground to walk there, click an object to use it, click a person to talk (keyboard
still works). **T** opens the quick-travel wheel: the smith walks the real road at speed and the clock advances by the
walking time. **K** opens the skill tree; **U** the skill levels. The bottom-left cards show your hirelings —
click one to look at them (or to hear where they are away to).

**Adventurers' character sheets** (Most Wanted board, J, or a dialogue's *Character sheet* button): overview,
combat, a paper-doll of every slot with durability bars, current expedition, career and ambitions, relationships
and history. Gear now wears out in real fights; broken gear works at half strength. Each morning adventurers look at
their kit and, by wealth, class and ambition, buy from your display, ask for repairs, commission pieces, buy from the
rival smith, buy a creature companion — or save up and risk it.

**Tailoring.** The new Textiles & Tailoring branch: spin flax and wool (loom), cut and sew (cutting table, tailor's
bench), weave silk, moonweave and spidersilk, and make tunics, gambesons, robes, cloaks and hoods — real magic and
ward for spellcasters. **More equipment:** shortswords, greatswords, cutlasses, rapiers, hatchets, battle and great
axes, war hammers, hide/studded/chain/half and full plate, bucklers and tower shields, short, recurve and war bows.

**Craftsmanship weights the dice.** Good work rolls the stat dice with advantage (two or three dice, best kept); poor
work with disadvantage. Physical dice keep your numbers and add a shown craftsmanship modifier. The result page
explains every stat.

**Customers** may bring several requests at once — something you cannot make yet, something you can, a repair: take
the parts you want. **The Hall of Fame** on the shopfront wall records masterworks, legendary finds, commissions and
deeds done with your work. A piece that helps slay a legend becomes **Epic** (its rarity unchanged): the village holds
a **ceremony in the square** — be there.

**Hirelings** can die on dangerous expeditions: a funeral, a grave in the churchyard, and sometimes a friendly ghost
who leaves wildflowers on your step for the vase in your quarters.

**New in the village:** the travelling caravan (north road), the Three Brass Balls pawnshop, Maren Tallowgate's rival forge, the Salt Anvil, and the Hales' menagerie (companion creatures for adventurers), horses
and cows, signs on every building, worn roads and street lamps. **Ironfront:** Barnaby invites you on day one.

## Iteration 011: the world remembers

**The world map (G, or right-click the Map button).** The lands about Brindlewick on parchment: every site the
adventurers have found, its danger, what lives there, what is left to gather, and its history. A unique master
slain stays dead — the map shows the lair cleared, and only a weaker successor may move in weeks later.

**Every piece has a story.** Right-click any weapon or armour to inspect it: the Overview shows your Ironhand mark,
the day and place it was forged, materials and craftsmanship; the Story tab lists every sale, battle, repair and
deed with links to the adventurers and places involved. Another smith's work keeps their mark.

**Names and Epic.** A blade that kills a legend earns a name of its own; a piece that does something remarkable
becomes Epic (its rarity unchanged). Famous creations hang on the Hall of Fame, the village talks about them, and
customers may commission "one just like it".

**A Familiar Creation.** When something you made comes back — for repair, for sale, as an heirloom of a fallen
adventurer or in a recovered chest — you recognise it, once, and see where it has been.

**Talking items.** Very rarely a piece wakes after a real event and starts to comment on the work at your bench.
Settings → Gameplay: *Item voices* on/off and *rare/normal* frequency.

## Iteration 012: the workshop comes alive

Once you can teach (Smithing 6, or as soon as you buy the first room next door), the **notice board** has a
*Workshop apprentices* tab. Take one on, then open the **Workshop Ledger (Y)** and give them work: customer repairs,
commissions, a run of longswords, smelting, or station upkeep. They work 8:00–18:00 at the real stations with the real
materials — you always have priority at a station. Talk to them to see how they are doing or to teach them for an hour.
Their pieces carry their own mark; they rise from novice to master and, one day, a master may want a workshop of their own.

## Never stuck: guidance, shops and improving your work

**What can I do, and what's missing?** In every station's recipe list each recipe says *Ready*, *Missing N* or *Locked*.
Press **How?** for the single next step (with a button that takes you there); open **Full details** for the whole chain —
for example Iron Longsword → Cured Leather → cure Raw Hide at a Leather Vat → buy hide at Pell's Provisions. Click any
missing ingredient, station or skill to open its page in **Help (F1)**.

**Shops.** The village map (**M**) lists every shop with its hours and whether it is open now; select one for a short
card and *Travel there*. **Pell's Provisions** sells everyday basics (iron ore, coal, hides, leather, linen, wool).
Counters close at night — the prompt tells you when they open.

**Improving a piece.** At the grinding wheel, anvil, engraving stand, stitching table or arcane anvil — or from an item's
inspection (*Improve it…*) — refine, rework, upgrade or reforge it. You see before and after, play the station's
precision check, and the piece keeps its name, maker and story; the work is written into its history.

## The Living Realm: a growing forge in a living world

**Your forge grows.** Each workshop room now shows its craft and gets finer as your skill, stations and renown grow.
When the leather room is full, buy **Ilse's Loft** next door: a second floor (stairs from the stock hall; later an
aetheric lift). After each step of a recipe, *Walk to …* takes you to the next station; *Craft again* repeats a recipe.

**Adventurers have lives.** Every campaign has its own world seed and roster. Adventurers chase their own goals, make
friends and rivals, form named companies, retire and are replaced. Look at their sheet (J, then a card): gear around a
figure, company, goals and bonds.

**Advice, sponsorship, contracts.** Adventurers sometimes come to the counter for advice — choose what to recommend; they
may refuse. In the Hirelings ledger (H) you can **sponsor** a company to fetch a material, a relic or lost gear from a
known site. Once your work is known, patrons write with **major contracts**: buy the **Project Bench** (Property ›
Equipment) and work in blocks of hours; apprentices can help.

## Quick evaluation with Developer Tools (F10)

The tools are locked until **Enable cheats for this session** at the top of the F10 panel. Cheats are never saved and switch off after a new game or a load; a red **CHEATS ON** banner shows while they are on, and a save made since is marked as tested with cheats. The same bar holds the optional **skill-check bypass** (every check scores 100% without the minigame); **Test scenarios** open the forge with materials, the workshop with an apprentice, a commission request at the counter or a contract at the Project Bench.

1. Add the **Smithing**, **Runecraft**, **Alchemy**, **Stitching**, **Gems** and **Relics** kits, plus +1000 gold, plus 2 levels in every craft.
2. Send Borin out from the ledger. **Hirelings: finish expeditions now** brings him home, and his bag appears.
3. Craft the Emberwarden. Put it on display, then **A customer sets out** (or talk to an adventurer).
4. **Send in-town adventurers out**, then **Adventurers: resolve every expedition now**. Read the outcome in the overview (J).
5. Use **Skip to 19:00** for the inn, **A dispute** for a refund-or-refuse decision, and **+20 affection** to speed up a romance.
6. Save from the menu, quit, Continue. Everything persists: employees, adventurers, crafted items, relationships and expeditions.

**Expeditions & difficulty (F10):** *Form, provision & dispatch parties now*, *Step every expedition once*, *Run every expedition to its end*, switch difficulty, pause, speed ×1/×4/×8, and open the board.

**A 10-minute test route for the living village:**

1. New Game → *Begin as Journeyman*. F10 → *+1000 gold*, *Materials* kit.
2. Forge an iron longsword (smelter → anvil → quench → grinding wheel). Put it on display (L → Stock) and sell it to an adventurer at the shop counter, or offer it in dialogue.
3. F10 → *Form, provision & dispatch parties now* (or wait until 06:00–08:00 next morning; parties sit at the inn tables).
4. Walk into the inn and press E at the MOST WANTED board by the door. Open the buyer's dossier: the sword is listed with its maker mark ⚒.
5. *Active Expeditions* → *Observe Expedition*. Press + a few times to speed time; watch the marker move and the journal grow. Inspect a fight.
6. Close the observer and keep playing; the expedition finishes without you.
7. *Legends & History* → *Revisit*. Check the shop counter for offers and the sword's history (hover it in the dossier).
8. Esc → Save, quit, Continue: the board, records and the sword are as you left them.
9. Esc → Difficulty → Master: *Observe* disappears and the board shows only departure times and rumours.
